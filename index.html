    THE FIELD NOTEBOOK

    A few useful connections.

    Built for the School of Tomorrow. Created by QSOL–IMC as independent contributors to open science. We are not members of the School of Tomorrow. This is an independent contribution, not an official School of Tomorrow publication or endorsement.

    Follow a coloured floor trail to an exhibit. The large E / Inspect button lights up when you are close enough. Press E or tap the button to begin. You can do the four experiments in any order. The compass points towards the next unfinished exhibit; the map shows the rooms and corridors.

    Fit window gives the station more room. Fullscreen uses your display when supported. Either transition releases held controls. Sound is optional: connections, experiments and the finale have their own original VANILLAJS chimes.

    For the finale, park both probes near the central engine. Q switches the probe you control; F turns it toward its partner. Face each other with a clear view, then hold Space for two seconds. Use the map to see both headings.

    Where the ideas come from

    Synergetics 95 — Educator Edition is the teaching reference and geometry source. Tetravolumes here use a regular tetrahedron of edge 1 as the unit. The equal-edge octahedron has volume 4; the cuboctahedron has volume 20. These comparisons do not mean that four regular tetrahedra tile a regular octahedron.

    Kirby Urner: The Proposal (Martian Math Portfolio) supplies the curriculum direction: make tetrahedral accounting and relationships something people can explore.

    Ryan’s RayShare supplies the raycaster and the two-observer connection idea. The relay is a game metaphor for sharing perspectives, not a physical law.

    Polydactica’s Earthbound Music playlist is a mood reference. Our score, A Small Department on a Very Large Planet, is original procedural music, made with VANILLAJS.

    Game code: Apache-2.0. RayShare adaptation: MIT. Synergetics 95 geometry: MPL-2.0. Source and retained notices are in the repository. This game explores Fuller’s geometry; it does not model Haken’s separate synergetics tradition.
